# Definition of Shipped

An app is NOT shipped until all three of these are true:

- **App Store approved** — the version is actually live and purchasable in the store, not just accepted into a review queue.
- **Paywall live** — your monetization offering is published, and the purchase flow has been tested end-to-end in a sandbox environment (paywall shown → purchase completed → entitlement granted → entitlement survives an app relaunch).
- **First install from a stranger** — at least one organic install from someone who doesn't know you personally.

"Code written," "submitted for review," and "waiting for review" all mean **in progress**, not shipped. It's easy to let momentum on the first two count as a finish line — they aren't one. A version sitting in review, or a paywall that renders in the simulator but has never been tested against a real sandbox purchase, is still an open task.

Every retrospective or wrap-up should mark progress against this three-part bar, not against commit counts, lines of code, or "the build compiled." Commit counts measure activity; this bar measures whether the thing you built actually reached a real user and actually makes money when it does.
